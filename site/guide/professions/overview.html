Professions in Syrolands ​

Design status: Proposed. This page describes the intended redesign, not confirmed live mechanics. Implementation/availability: not established for this redesign. Verification: documentation review only. Reviewed: 2026-10-08 against the full design specification.

Crafts belong to adventurers ​

“There are no crafter characters; there are characters who know crafts.” Professions should matter while exploring, surviving, maintaining equipment, fighting where appropriate and participating in the economy—not only while standing in a workshop.

They share the character's overall skill budget. Knowing a craft should create useful choices rather than supply a generic combat buff.

Learning and becoming known ​

The proposed path begins with a starting competency or basic instruction, followed by practice and discovery. Low skill should already be useful. The direction is roughly a 700-point total cap and 100 normal maximum, with increasingly slow gains near mastery; these are not finalized settings. Endgame beyond 100 comes from knowledge, techniques, reputation and player understanding.

A skill is broad capability. A technique is learned knowledge, such as Bowmaking or Distillation. A profession path combines skill, techniques and practice. Your identity is what others know you for: perhaps especially durable war bows or beautiful garments.

You do not need seven skills at 100 to be viable. Supporting skills and practical knowledge should make hybrid characters worthwhile.

Knowledge and production ​

Each character should build a profession journal through observation, teachers, books, exploration and experiments. Reaching 100 would not reveal everything. Shared advice helps, but materials and execution still matter: knowledge can be shared; mastery cannot be copied.

Manual work establishes understanding. Known procedures may then support time-consuming production batches at a station, with real materials and interruptions. Commissions should ask for useful results, and reputation should reflect what you make well rather than a single rating.

Item Quality / Masterworks explains why good materials, technique and execution should matter more than a lucky roll. Skills, world protections, exact learning rules and economic balance still need reconciliation before implementation.

Shared character rules ​

Character progression, skills and teachers, techniques and backgrounds explain the shared framework. Profession-specific discovery, production and reputation build on it.

Inscription and magical knowledge ​

Inscription extends the Proposed shared framework to scrolls, variants, grimoires and notation. Learning/copying permissions remain distinct from crafting capability; physical materials and valuable books retain loot risk.
