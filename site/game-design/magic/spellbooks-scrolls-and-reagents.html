Spellbooks, scrolls and reagents ​

Design status: Proposed; listed decisions remain Unresolved. Existing Accepted foundations retain their scope. Implementation: no Batch 04 gameplay implementation established. Availability: not established for this redesign. Verification: documentation reconciliation only, not runtime validation. Reviewed: 2026-10-09 against Batch 04 and earlier magic design.

Player guide · Magic overview · Status and open questions

Knowledge belongs to the character ​

The proposed teaching interaction is to present the personal spellbook, have the teacher inscribe the technique and permanently learn it. Teachers should not normally dispense tradable permanent-learning scrolls.

Permanent knowledge is authoritative server-side character state. The personal book represents and interfaces with it; it is not the sole persistence store. The earlier proposal considered blessing or another protection mechanism. Batch 04 narrows the direction to a baseline personal knowledge container that should not normally drop; it does not select blessing, Sacred status or general item insurance as the mechanism. Exceptional book loss must not erase knowledge; a valid replacement should recover its representation.

Unresolved interaction with loot: equipment design favors meaningful loss and does not approve general insurance. Personal-book protection is a proposed specific exception, not acceptance of Sacred Item retention, protection for every crafted grimoire or a change to existing death rules. Knowledge recovery, book replacement and the fate of prepared modifiers/material value require explicit design before implementation. Permanent magical knowledge is also distinct from Slice 0's intentionally transient injury state; no save schema is selected here.

Losing months of discovery in one death would disproportionately punish mages relative to martial characters. Protecting learned knowledge does not protect the market value of every book. A special physical grimoire/manuscript can be found, bought, sold, stolen, dropped, looted and studied. It may contain rare patterns, modifiers, schools, annotations or reference benefits. The exact boundary between personal representation and a valuable physical item is unresolved.

Scrolls provide temporary access ​

A scroll is a consumable, pre-structured execution that may cast an unlearned spell without permanently teaching it. Common scrolls can make early magic accessible at an economic cost; they must not bypass exploration and institutional learning.

Possible benefits include a lower Magery floor, improved success, preconfigured execution or different timing. None is guaranteed for all scrolls. Advanced scrolls may require high Inscription, the scribe's own knowledge, rare materials and recipes; some restricted/forbidden techniques may not be encodable on ordinary scrolls. Availability, restrictions and consumption on failure remain open.

Batch 04 additionally proposes scrolls as physical representations of patterns, variant-specific tools and rapid-reference access outside Active Memory. A known but unmemorized Dispel scroll might reduce or bypass part of lookup delay in an emergency, at the cost of consuming the scroll. This does not set actual casting time, universal eligibility bypass or consumption-on-failure behavior.

Teaching-scroll conflict: the earlier proposal says teachers should not normally distribute tradable permanent-learning scrolls and copying must not recreate that market. Batch 04 allows a manuscript/learning artifact only if explicitly designed for that purpose. Ordinary casting scrolls remain temporary access; a distinct teaching-artifact policy is Unresolved. No automatic permanent learning, unrestricted copying or generic exception is approved.

Reagents remain physical resources ​

Retain reagents as catalysts, stabilizers, carriers and material components for patterns/modifiers, rather than only arbitrary legacy recipe tokens. Modifiers may add or substitute reagents, alter catalyst requirements or increase material costs. Alchemy can prepare/process materials; Inscription uses them in encoded structures. Exact families, recipes and substitution rules are unresolved. No legacy reagent is removed or redesigned in runtime.

Grimoire workmanship ​

A well-made physical book may improve search/reference speed, organization, variant-notation stability, indexed-variant number/quality and owner notes. An Indexed Grimoire illustrates faster reference rather than a flat “+10 Magery” item. Such rare physical benefits remain exposed to loot risk unless a later explicit rule says otherwise. Inscription owns production, with item quality supplying shared principles.

Open questions ​

Personal-book replacement and physical/knowledge boundary; rare grimoire loss, theft and study; scroll eligibility, timing and consumption on success/fizzle/interruption; casting versus teaching artifacts; reagent families and substitution; prepared variant material value and indexing limits. No save, inventory or loot implementation is selected.
