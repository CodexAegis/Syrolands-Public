Inscription ​

Design status: Proposed; listed decisions remain Unresolved. Existing Accepted foundations retain their scope. Implementation: no Batch 04 gameplay implementation established. Availability: not established for this redesign. Verification: documentation reconciliation only, not runtime validation. Reviewed: 2026-10-09 against Batch 04 and earlier magic design.

Player guide · Magic overview · Status and open questions

Inscription as magical engineering ​

A master scribe records, modifies, preserves, prepares and embeds magical structures rather than simply printing scrolls. Candidate domains are:

  • Scroll crafting, potentially requiring personal knowledge of advanced spells.
  • Spell augmentations, glyphs and formulae.
  • Runes or glyphs on objects, doors, houses, terrain and rituals.
  • Prepared wards and protections.
  • Specialized spellbook/grimoire structures.
  • Encoded ritual components.
  • Preservation and copying only where explicitly allowed by design.

Copying must not silently recreate a tradable permanent-learning market. Exact production rules and recipes are unfinished.

Batch 04 expands written magical structure into controlled transformation and transmission: variant scrolls, glyph construction, stable modifier design, grimoire writing/binding, indexing/reference optimization, wards, ritual diagrams, notation and preservation of rare schools/techniques. These are candidate activities, not a final craft menu or working ward system.

Skill, technique and profession path ​

Use the shared Skill → Technique/School → Profession Path model. Inscription provides execution capability; learned methods and access to knowledge determine what can be encoded. Magery does not automatically grant every pattern, and Inscription skill alone does not imply ownership of rare knowledge.

Basic Spell Scribing, Glyph Construction, Advanced Indexing, Ward Inscription, Ritual Diagramming, Variant Stabilization and Grimoire Binding are illustrative techniques. A scribe might specialize in combat scrolls, rare variant preservation, wards, ritual notation or high-quality grimoires. No giant final tree, extra elemental skills or automatic unlock thresholds are selected.

Knowledge, production and economy ​

The Profession System owns journals/Codex, secrets, partial teaching, manual discovery, batch production, commissions and reputation. Apply those shared questions to magical notation without inventing a separate production minigame, permanent recipe unlock or new economic service.

Magery, known patterns, techniques/schools, reagents and Alchemy can interact with Inscription. Ritual/religious dependencies are possible later, not required systems now. Research and preservation should give scribes economic roles beyond selling skill bonuses: emergency scroll access, dependable variants, good indexing and rare notation. Reputation may reflect useful products and provenance under the shared profession direction, not a finalized rating formula.

Scroll reference benefits and material risk preserve consumable costs and loot exposure. Copying/transmission must respect the unresolved teaching-artifact boundary; player teaching is not unrestricted permanent spell duplication. Rare techniques and secrets need abuse-resistant custody and discovery rules before implementation.

Workmanship and reference quality ​

Grimoire organization, stable notation, indexed variants and owner notes may improve reference/preparation. Avoid flat “book grants +10 Magery” design. Crafted and dropped books should fit shared quality and horizontal itemization; neither craftsmanship nor Sacred/Resonant terminology grants death protection.

Authority and technical limits ​

The server validates knowledge, prerequisites, materials, ownership, work, consumption and results. The client presents writing, book navigation and feedback; animations do not finish a craft or authorize a cast. No engine changes, recipe tables, item classes, persistence schemas or packets are selected.

Open questions ​

Final technique roster; craft and variant prerequisites; copy/teaching permissions; notation secrecy/provenance; production and interruption/material rules; ward/ritual scope; binding/indexing benefits; quality and reputation abuse; relationship to future ritual/religious systems. All implementation and balancing require separate scope.
