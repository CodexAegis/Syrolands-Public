Tinkering ​

Design status: Proposed; all mechanics, examples and technique paths below remain candidates. Implementation: no implementation of this redesign is established by this documentation batch. Availability: not established. Verification: documentation review only; no gameplay test. Reviewed: 2026-10-09 for public projection; design status and mechanics preserved from the canonical specification.

Player-facing overview · Profession System

Purpose and profession loop ​

Tinkering is precision mechanics / engineering / mechanisms.

Core loop ​

Inspect -> Design -> Assemble -> Calibrate -> Test -> Maintain

Identity: Tinkering is a deliberately "nerdy" profession that supports many specialized gameplay families.

Abstract mechanism model ​

  • Power
  • Motion
  • Trigger
  • Control
  • Output

Possible component/logic families ​

Inputs/triggers:

  • switch;
  • pressure plate;
  • tripwire;
  • timer;
  • contact;
  • other mechanical triggers.

Logic/control:

  • simple AND/OR/NOT-like behavior;
  • delays;
  • counters;
  • relays;

but keep it intentionally bounded.

Outputs:

  • doors;
  • gates;
  • cages;
  • bells;
  • feeders;
  • pulleys;
  • traps;
  • lights;
  • dispensers;
  • other mechanisms.

Housing automation ​

Tinkering should eventually allow players to create mechanical logic systems inside housing, conceptually similar to a constrained redstone/logic-gate layer:

  • switch opens gate;
  • timer activates feeder;
  • motion/pressure trigger activates alarm;
  • mechanical sequence operates cage/pulley;

etc.

Housing automation is a future proposal, outside implementation scope.

Locks/security ​

Tinkering can create and inspect locks/mechanical security. Lockpicking defeats them. Tinkering + Lockpicking provides richer understanding.

Housing should not necessarily be magically impenetrable. Future burglary gameplay may include:

  • inspecting locks;
  • lockpicking outer doors;
  • hidden/stealth movement;
  • Detect Hidden for traps/alarms;
  • Remove Trap/Tinkering for countermeasures;
  • locked internal containers;
  • alarm countdowns;
  • guard/security response;
  • risk escalation.

Balance must prevent trivial AFK lockpicking/total house loss.

Traps ​

Modular combination of trigger + mechanism + output/payload. Alchemy can provide payloads. Tinkering provides control/delivery.

Reverse engineering ​

  • inspect;
  • disassemble;
  • infer mechanism;
  • reconstruct;
  • risk damage;
  • discover techniques from world devices.

Dungeon utility ​

  • repair mechanisms;
  • restore lifts/bridges;
  • bypass mechanical systems;
  • diagnose traps;
  • operate machinery.

Golems / mechanical automatons ​

Tinkering should eventually support high-end mechanical constructs.

Possible progression:

  • simple toys/devices;
  • utility automatons;
  • pack/maintenance constructs;
  • stationary mechanisms;
  • combat-capable constructs;
  • true mechanical golems as high-end specialization.

Large constructs may require collaboration:

  • Blacksmithing for frame/armor;
  • Tinkering for movement/control;
  • Alchemy for power/lubricants/volatile systems;
  • possibly Inscription/Magery for magical control/binding, if later accepted.

Golems remain a future high-end specialization direction; detailed design is deferred.

Knowledge and specialization ​

Mechanist's Journal. World devices can teach techniques through inspection and risky reverse engineering. Precision mechanics, security and future constructs provide distinct specialist identities.

Shared progression, production and economy ​

The character skill framework owns teaching and cap/gain direction. The Profession System owns craft discovery, knowledge sharing, manual mastery, batch production, commissions and multidimensional reputation. Those rules are not independently redefined here. The quality specification owns the shared result envelope and rare item properties.

Related skills and field interactions ​

Lockpicking defeats locks while Tinkering understands/builds them; together they provide richer understanding. Detect Hidden and Remove Trap may support burglary counterplay. Alchemy supplies payload/power/lubricant candidates; Blacksmithing supplies frames, Carpentry structures, and future Magery/Inscription may provide control only if accepted.

Alchemy · Blacksmithing · Carpentry · Magery and Inscription · Protection and regeneration.

Abstraction and technical boundary ​

Keep only state and feedback that support meaningful decisions. The shared abstraction/authority constraints and living-world architecture apply; persistence requires a separate technical design. Nothing here selects packet IDs, schemas, timers, UI assets or runtime algorithms.

Open questions ​

Limits on logic chains, timers and active mechanisms; housing permissions and burglary losses; traps, detection, alarms and counterplay; reverse-engineering loss and knowledge; construct control, costs and persistence. Golems remain a high-end direction without recipes, stats, AI or a chosen magical binding system.

The shared reconciliation register owns cross-system conflicts. Existing combat experiments and evidence do not validate this profession redesign.
