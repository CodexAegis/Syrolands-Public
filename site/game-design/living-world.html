Living world and world materials ​

Design status: Proposed. Implementation: the supplied draft describes an unimplemented system; no source or runtime verification of this system was performed during integration.

Recorded 2026-10-04 as a gameplay proposal. Simulation, persistence, networking and the candidate prototype remain future technical work; this proposal does not change the current combat experiment.

Existing server authority remains Accepted. The living-world pillar, material rules, examples, lifecycles and profession extensions below remain Proposed. Numbers and named effects illustrate intent rather than final balance or an established content catalogue.

Identity and systemic interaction ​

The world should visibly remember meaningful actions. Nature has state; materials matter; professions physically change the landscape; magic interacts with the environment; traces and terrain create tactical choices; recovery prevents ordinary play from irreversibly exhausting the world. The target is systemic believability rather than maximum physical realism. Each feature must justify its server cost through visible, understandable gameplay.

The draft proposes this as a major design pillar alongside localized anatomy, systemic combat, material-based equipment and redesigned Magery. This is a proposed relationship, not acceptance of every adjacent system.

Actions supply reusable stimuli, and affected materials resolve their responses:

ActionStimulusPossible material response
FireballHeat, ignition potential, fire damage and localized environmental energyVegetation ignites, wood scorches, ice melts, or resistant material remains unchanged
PickaxeImpact, mining force and structural damageStone resists, chips, cracks, becomes rubble or reveals an authored passage
AxeCutting force, material damage and directional interactionWood reacts according to species, integrity, hardness, moisture, tool quality and skill

A spell should not contain a separate special case for every tree, grass or wall. These examples describe a common interaction model, not a committed API or permission to replace legacy behavior globally.

Materials ​

Candidate families are Wood, Vegetation, Stone, Soil, Mud, Water, Ice, Cloth, Metal, Bone, Organic/Flesh and Ash. Objects expose only useful properties: hardness, structural integrity, flammability, ignition threshold, moisture, heat retention, conductivity, brittleness, burn duration, regeneration behavior, passability and destruction products. Prefer data-driven definitions where practical; not every object needs every property.

World-material structural integrity is a separate concept from combat body-region integrity. No shared numerical scale or conversion to Local Trauma is established by this draft.

Trees and forestry ​

A candidate lifecycle is:

text
Healthy → Notched → Heavily Chopped → Falling → Fallen Tree / Logs
→ Stump → Cleared Ground → Regrowth → Sapling → Young Tree → Mature Tree

Species, age, integrity, moisture, size, wood quality, current state and regrowth schedule may affect the result. Standing trees block movement in the proposed interaction; a falling tree might be a temporary hazard. A stump may block or partially restrict movement until removed. Exact footprints and hazard rules remain unresolved rather than requirements for every legacy tree graphic.

This ties lumber supply and forest density to physical changes. Repeated harvesting could turn dense forest into a clearing and a practical route over several days; that duration is illustrative. Forestry management, rare species, overharvesting, recovery, disease, planting, forest fires and wildlife dependence are later extensions.

Fire, moisture and weather ​

Fire is the proposed flagship demonstration. Candidate sources include campfires, Fireball, Fire Field, dragon breath, alchemical explosions, burning projectiles, lava, lightning, burning structures and neighboring fire.

Ignition considers flammability, dryness, applied heat, nearby fire intensity and wind, opposed by moisture, rain and fire resistance. This is a list of influences, not an additive formula, probability model or fixed wind bonus. Direction and local circumstances still matter.

Candidate vegetation states are Healthy, Dry, Igniting, Burning, Smoldering, Burned, Ash and Recovering. Trees might become Scorched, Burning, Charred, Collapsed and Burned Stump. These are vocabulary for later state design, not a complete transition graph.

Moisture may depend on biome, rainfall, nearby water, terrain, recent weather, shade, season and temperature. An illustrative scale runs from 0.0 extremely dry to 1.0 saturated. Dry grass ignites readily; wet grass resists; wet wood burns poorly; suitable soil becomes mud after rain; wet terrain can stop a fire; drought raises regional risk. Region/chunk values with local exceptions are preferred over continuously ticking every tile.

A proposed shared server weather state supplies wind direction/strength, humidity, rainfall and temperature. Fire, smoke and hazards use these inputs; later candidates include projectiles, sailing and scent/tracking. Client foliage, waves, rain, smoke, particles and shaders should reflect the same conditions. This draft does not establish an implemented weather service or choose global, regional or biome-local weather.

In the draft's wildfire example, low humidity and dry vegetation let Fireball start a fire that travels westward. That interpretation requires wind from the east; “Strong East” alone is ambiguous. Wind direction conventions remain open. Water magic and physical firebreaks are candidate responses; intentional firebreak mechanics are not yet decided.

Terrain and authored geology ​

Terrain changes should use temporary or persistent states without rewriting the base map for each interaction. Candidate states include Grass, Dry Grass, Burned Grass, Ash, Soil, Mud, Snow, Ice, Flooded, Rubble and Cleared Ground.

StateProposed gameplay consequences
MudSlower movement, visible footprints and possible tracking advantage
SnowSlower movement, clear footprints and later cold exposure
IceReduced traction and possible slipping
Burning terrainDamage, ignition and possible smoke-related visibility obstruction
AshTemporary visibility changes, tracking clues and a regeneration phase

Introduce these incrementally. Movement, damage, concealment and visibility outcomes remain server decisions; cosmetic smoke alone does not establish a gameplay visibility rule.

Rock might progress through Solid → Damaged → Cracked → Rubble → Cleared / Passable. Applications include resource veins, blocked tunnels, hidden routes, weak-rock collapse, shortcuts and dungeon traversal. Start with authored destructible or transformable elements at known map positions or controlled layers. A hidden cave example means an authored inaccessible passage becomes reachable, not unrestricted voxel digging.

Magic and tactical preparation ​

The magic redesign and world materials should share environmental interactions:

InfluenceCandidate effects
FireIgnite vegetation, dry surfaces, melt ice, damage wood and produce smoke
WaterExtinguish fire, increase moisture, form mud and reduce ignition
ColdSuppress combustion, freeze water and create ice
LightningIgnite dry vegetation and interact with conductive materials or water
WindMove smoke and amplify or suppress effects according to direction
EarthDamage or reinforce terrain, create temporary obstacles and interact with stone/mining

These are examples of tactical consequences, not a final spell list, elemental taxonomy or implementation authorization. Siege preparation could include clearing concealment or wetting defensive ground. Whether particular areas permit those actions depends on unresolved protection rules.

Knowledge, professions and traces ​

Information should depend on skills, tools, context and profession rather than exposing every simulation value numerically. Ordinary observation might report dry grass; expertise might identify high fire risk and wind conditions, an oak near falling, or fractured rock at risk of collapse. Precision, checks and eligibility remain open.

Profession or skillProposed contribution
LumberjackingEfficiency, species knowledge, safe/directional felling and yield
MiningStructural analysis, material identification, safe excavation and rare resources
MageryEnvironmental manipulation through shared material rules
TrackingInterpretation of footprints and disturbed terrain
CampingControlled fire, safety and fuel efficiency
Anatomy, Tactics and Arms LorePrimarily combat expertise, sharing the broader knowledge-driven philosophy

Survival, Forestry, Engineering and Geology are possible future skills only if they add meaningful play. This proposal adds no skills and changes no existing cap or approved Anatomy rule.

Later tracking evidence could include footprints, broken vegetation, blood, disturbed mud, displaced ash, dragged objects and animal tracks. Low skill might reveal only that tracks exist; high skill might infer a heavy human, approximate age and northwest travel. The draft's fifteen-minute example is illustrative precision, not an accepted disclosure rule. Rain erases evidence, mud preserves prints, snow records movement, wind removes light traces and fire destroys many traces; exact lifetimes remain unresolved.

Ecology as a future layer ​

Vegetation can support herbivores; herbivores support predators; water shapes routes; forest density provides shelter. Fire damages habitat, overharvesting creates scarcity and regrowth restores resources. A proposed causal example is reduced forest density → fewer deer → less wolf food → migration → more encounters near farms. This expresses future systemic consequences rather than guaranteed population equations or a conventional quest trigger.

Full ecology and wildlife migration are excluded from the initial prototype. Existing creatures do not gain these behaviors by documentation integration.

Protection and regeneration ​

Mutable wilderness, towns, guarded zones, landmarks, housing, event areas, dungeons and scripted spaces need distinct policies. Candidate safeguards include blocking spread into protected town tiles, fast road recovery, immutable landmarks, resource permissions, controlled house destructibility, criminal consequences for arson and restrictions near newcomers. None of these policies is selected yet. Building destruction is outside the first version.

Grass and trees regrow, ash clears, mud dries, wildlife repopulates and renewable resources recover. Rates may depend on biome, weather, season, player activity and world events. Intentionally authored changes may be permanent or semi-permanent; ordinary play should not permanently ruin the world. Persistent memory and restoration must be balanced deliberately.

Open design questions ​

  • Which areas and objects are mutable, and how do housing, construction, crime and permissions interact?
  • How quickly do trees regrow and wilderness recover? Which changes deliberately remain permanent?
  • Can falling trees damage creatures, and how do stump, log and rubble footprints work?
  • Can players deliberately create firebreaks, and what actions create one?
  • What can each skill disclose, with what precision and tools?
  • Is wind global, regional or biome-local, and does its direction mean “from” or “toward”?

Simulation cadence, chunk sizes, dormant fires, storage, NPC navigation and destructive-action auditing remain technical open questions. The candidate prototype and verification gates are future work, not the current roadmap commitment.

Profession Batch 01 integration ​

Mining, Lumberjacking, Carpentry and Tinkering now detail the Proposed profession loops. Cooking connects to Camping/fire; Alchemy connects preparations to material interactions. Shared progression and knowledge belong to the Profession System.

This batch places geological, forestry and engineering expertise inside existing broad skill directions; the earlier possible standalone skills above remain unreconciled candidates, not additions to a decided roster. Mining remains subject to authored geology, and obstruction, recovery, housing security and bridges remain subject to unresolved protection rules. No expansion of the initial tree/grass prototype is authorized.

Source and status boundary ​

This proposal supplies no new Accepted decision, implementation proof, performance measurements or gameplay validation.
